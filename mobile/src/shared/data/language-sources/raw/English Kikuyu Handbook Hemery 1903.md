## PREFACE.

THE rapid development of British East Africa brought about by the Uganda Railway, and also by the encouragement given to settlers, and traders of all nations and tribes, by the Consul-General, Sir Charles Eliot, renders every day more urgent the necessity of learning the Principal Dialects spoken by the natives of this important part of Africa.

Numerous hand-books have already appeared in the Kiswahili dialect, which is the most important of all, and which, one day, will probably supplant all the others.

We now offer to the Officers, Missionaries, Traders and Settlers, working in the beautiful and fertile province of KIKUYU, the first rough sketch of the language spoken by the natives of this district. We are aware of the many defects of this little work; it probably contains many mistakes, but such as it is, we are confident it will prove useful to all, and hope it may be the forerunner of some work that will be more scientific and more complete.

**A. HEMERY, C.S.Sp.**

## THE ALPHABET.

The alphabet, in the Kikuyu language, is the same as in Kiswahili.

- *ch*, *sh*, and *w*, as in English.
- *g*, as g in gate.
- *j*, as in French, jour.
- *s*, as in sun, English.
- *t*, as in ten, English.
- *y*, as in yes, English.

The vowels sound as in Italian.

The letter *f* is not used.

N.B.—The natives confuse very often the following sounds :—

- *l* and *r*
- *g* and *k*
- *sh* and *ch*
- *o* and *u*
- *e* and *i*

# THE GRAMMAR.

## CHAPTER I.
### THE NOUN.

There is no gender, properly speaking, in Kikuyu.

Two things are to be distinguished in nouns : their class, their number.

There are nine classes of nouns. There are two numbers, singular and the plural.

Some nouns belong to one class for the singular, to another for the plural.

#### I. Class.

The first class comprises the nouns beginning in the singular with *m-*, *mu-*, and *mo-*, which form their plural by changing *m-*, *mu-*, *mo-* into *a-*. They all refer to rational beings. Ex. :—

| Singular | Meaning | Plural |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Mundu | *a* Man | *pl.* Andu |
| Mkikuyu | *a* Kikuyu | *pl.* AKikuyu |
| Mzungu | *an* European | *pl.* Azungu |
| Mnene | *a* Chief | *pl.* Anene |
| Moireto | *a* Girl | *pl.* Aireto |

#### II. Class.

Nouns which form their plural by changing *m-* into *me-*. Ex. :—

| Singular | Meaning | Plural |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Mte, *or* Mote | *a* Tree | *pl.* Mete |
| Mweri | *the* Moon | *pl.* Meeri |
| Motoe | *the* Head | *pl.* Metoe |
| Mwere | *the* Body | *pl.* Meere |
| Mkiha | *the* Vein | *pl.* Mekiha |
| Mrigo | *a* Load | *pl.* Merigo |
| Mwaka | *a* Year | *pl.* Meaka |
| Mgunda | *a* Field | *pl.* Megunda |
| Mtito | *a* Forest | *pl.* Metito |

### DIMINUTIVES AND AUGMENTATIVES.

In Kikuyu, as in Kiswahili, nouns have a diminutive and an augmentative form.

Diminutives are formed by prefixing *ka-* to the noun; augmentatives by prefixing *ke-*. e.g. :—

| Type | Base Noun | Meaning | Derived Form | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *Dimin.* | Ngondu | *a* sheep | Kangondu | *a* small sheep |
| | Munyu | salt | Kamunyu | *a* little salt |
| | Ngombe | *an* ox | Kangombe | *a* little ox |
| *Augm.* | Ngombe | *an* ox | Kengombe | *a* big ox |
| | Munyu | salt | Kemunyu | plenty of salt |

There is no article in Kikuyu.

## CHAPTER IV.
### THE PRONOUN.

#### Art. I.—Personal Pronouns.

Pronouns are either disjunctive or conjunctive.
Disjunctive pronouns are not joined with other words.
Conjunctive pronouns are those joined with the verb, forming a single word with it, and denoting either the subject or the object of the sentence.

##### *§ 1.—Personal disjunctive pronouns.*

(*a*) Those of the 1st and 2nd person are the same for all classes of nouns, they are the following :—

| *Sing.* | Pronoun | *Pl.* | Pronoun |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| I or me | *niye* | we | *isue* |
| Thou | *we* | you | *inyue* |

(*b*) Those of the third person change according to the class of nouns they refer to, as :—

| Singular | Plural |
| :--- | :--- |
| he, *ake* | they, *wao* |
| „ *a* | „ *mao* |
| „ *e* | „ *meo* |
| „ *kya* | „ *shio* |
| „ *rya* | „ *mao*, *etc., etc.* |

*N.B.—of*, denoting *possession*, is expressed by *-a* added to the disjunctive pronoun proper to the possessor, as :—

#### Art. II.—Possessive Pronouns.

Possessive pronouns are the same as the possessive adjectives. They agree with the nouns in the same way as the adjectives, according to the different classes. e.g. :—

| Kikuyu | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| *-akwa* | Mine or my |
| *-aku* | Thine or thy |
| *-ake* | His or her |
| *-ito* | Ours or our |
| *-anyu* | Yours or your |
| *-ao* | Theirs or their |

which make, *makwa*, *shiakwa*, *ryakwa*, *waku*, *shito*, *wanyu*, *shiao*, according to the different classes.

#### Art. III.—Demonstrative pronouns.

They are the same as the demonstrative adjectives.
They are of three kinds :—
(1) Referring to objects that are *near*.
(2) Referring to objects that are *distant*.
(3) Referring to objects spoken of beforehand.

They change according to the classes of the nouns to which they refer, as :

| Class | Number | Noun | 1 (Near) | 2 (Distant) | 3 (Beforehand) | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |

| | | | | etc., etc., etc. | | |

#### Art. IV.—Relative pronouns.

| Kikuyu | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| *- o -*, | who, what, which. |
| *mwene*, | this who. |

#### Art. V.—Interrogative pronouns.

| Kikuyu | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| *Nou* or *ou?* | who? *oreko*, *areko*, *kereko*, etc. |
| *Ke?* *Neke?* | what? which? what is that? |

#### Art. VI.—Indefinite pronouns.

*One*, *People*, etc., is translated by *mundu*, *andu*, as :—

> mundu ne aoka = somebody comes.

*Ngutire*, *Hatire* = there is not, there is none.

It is used by itself, or followed by a noun; and may refer to living beings or things, as :—

| Kikuyu | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| Ngutire nguadje, | There are no sweet potatoes. |
| Hatire kendo, | There is nothing. |

*One*, *The other*, etc., are translated by, *mwe*, *ungi*, as :—

> Omwe alutaga wera, ungi ne aikara se. One works, another sits down.

## CHAPTER V.
### THE VERB.

There is only *one* Conjugation in Kikuyu.
There are *five* Moods, viz. :—
- The *infinitive*.
- The *imperative*.
- The *indicative*.
- The *subjunctive*.
- The *conditional*.

*Nota.*—The subjunctive is often used instead of the imperative.

#### Art. II.—Different forms of the same verb.

##### I.—Determinative Form.

By changing the final *-a* of the root into *-era*, e.g. :—

| Simple | Meaning | Determinative | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Koluta | To do | Kolutera | To do to |
| Kokinya | To reach | Kokinyera | To reach at, etc. |

##### II.—Passive Form.

By changing generally the final *-a* of the root into *-wo*, or *-ilwo*, e.g. :—

| Active | Meaning | Passive | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Kohora | To beat | Kohorwo | To be beaten |
| Koona | To see | Koonwo | To be seen |

##### III.—Causative Form.

By changing the final *-a* into *-ia*, or *-ya*, as :—

| Neuter | Meaning | Causative | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Kohona | To cure (neuter) | Kohonya | To cure (active) |
| Koikoroka | To descend (n.) | Koikorokia | To bring down |

##### IV.—Reflexive Form.

By prefixing *-e-* to the root of the verb, as :—

| Simple / Reflexive | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| Kooraga<br>Ko*e*oraga | To kill,<br>To kill one's self. |
| Kogora<br>Ko*e*gora | To buy,<br>To buy one's self. |

##### V.—Reciprocal Form.

By adding *-na*, or *-ana*, to the verb, as :—

| Simple / Reciprocal | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| Kokwenda<br>Kokwendana | To love,<br>To love each other. |

*Kolaya* = far, far off, far away, etc.
> Ex. :—Keshwrine ne kolaya, &emsp; The coast is far away.

*Hange* = elsewhere, out.
> Ex. :—Sie koina hange, &emsp; Go and play elsewhere.

*Igoro*, *Logoro* = up, upon, over, etc.
> Ex. :—Ndahuma logoro, &emsp; I come from above.

*Se*, *ze* = down under, underneath, below, beneath, etc.
> Ex. :—Oka se, &emsp; Come down.

*Reu*, *reu-reu* = now, presently, immediately, to-day, etc.
> Ex. :—Ndainuka aeu, &emsp; I go back presently.

*Rochio* = to-morrow. *Oke* = the day after to-morrow. *Ira* = yesterday. *Iyo* = the day before yesterday.

*Hingo shiose* = often, frequently, etc.

*-le*, affixed to the verb, is equivalent to our *when*, *as*, *if*.
> Ndalutara wera*le* negokwata rupia, If I do work I shall get Rupees.

*Tya?* *atya?* *atyatya?* = How? How is it? etc.
*-tya* is also affixed to verbs. Waeka*tya*. What do you do?

*Rochine* = early, early in the morning.

*Rochine tene.* Very early.

*Kanini*, *tonini*, etc. = a little, very little.
> Ex. :—Lehe kamchele kanini, &emsp; Give me a little rice.

*He*, *e*, *oguo*, *neguo* = yes, all right, certainly, etc.

*Acha*, *ngutire*, *hatire* = no, none, nothing, etc.

*Na* = With, by, etc.
> Ex. :—Ndasie nawe, &emsp; I go with you.

*Noo* = but, etc.

*Kana* = if, when.
> Ex. :—Ndimenya *kana* ne agobka, &emsp; I do not know if he will come.

*Kana* = or, either.
> Ex. :—Ne mburi, kana ne ngondu? &emsp; Is it a goat or a sheep?

*Hihi* = perhaps.

*Aderere* = to call for attention before talking. It means :—
> Listen, listen to me.

*Ti*, negative. Ex. :—Ti mwega, &emsp; It is not good.

*Ne muhoro*, *muhoro* = good day, good morning. You say in reply : *Nde muhoro*, *muhoro mweg*.

*Tiguo uhoro*, *tigwoni uhoro* = good-bye, farewell.
